\abstract{% NOTE: ~200 words, mock-up — rewrite in your own voice before submission.
Multi-source DC power systems such as hybrid fuel-cell/battery vehicles require controllable power sharing between their sources, yet the established droop-control literature presumes custom converters whose inner control loops the designer owns. This paper shows that commanded, continuously variable droop can instead be obtained from unmodified off-the-shelf boost regulators by perturbing each converter's feedback node with a multiplying DAC driven from shunt current sense, synthesizing an electronically programmable output resistance. Because the resulting plant inherits wide vendor tolerance bands and channel asymmetry, the outer power-share loop is closed by a robust $H_\infty$/Youla-parameterized controller synthesized against a 60-corner uncertain plant family rather than tuned at nominal. The controller achieves exact DC tracking, an 11.7~ms delay margin, and a worst-corner sensitivity peak of 1.87, compared with 26.9 for a nominally tuned PI; an independent full-order converter model validates the design plant to within 6\% across 432 operating points. Hardware bring-up failures, diagnosed through parasitic-inductance extraction from fabrication data and ideal-diode soft-start timing analysis, are shown to be generic multi-converter hazards rather than consequences of the droop architecture. The approach delivers accurate, tolerance-immune power sharing without a custom power stage.}
