\subsection{Discretization and Anti-Windup}
\label{sec:antiwindup}
Because the integrator Youla-H plants in $G_{CYH}$ is an \emph{exact} pole at $s=0$,
the controller separates uniquely into an integrator and a strictly stable remainder,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:split}
G_{CYH}(s) = \frac{k_I}{s} + R(s), \qquad
k_I = K_{YH}\frac{\prod_{i} z_i}{\prod_{p_j\neq 0} p_j}.
\end{equation}
No such split exists for $G_{CH}$, whose integral action is buried in the slow pole
pair of Equation \ref{eq:Gch}. The split makes the digital realization well posed:
the integrator is discretized by the trapezoidal rule as an explicit scalar state,
and $R(s)$ is balance-truncated (which requires a stable system) and discretized by
Tustin's method \citep{tustin1947method}. The explicit integrator state also admits the
standard PI anti-windup schemes, back-calculation or conditional integration, directly.

Figure \ref{fig:YH-AW-18} applies this to the drivetrain system with a $5$\,m/s step
and an illustrative limit $|T_e|\le 0.1$\,N$\cdot$m, chosen to rail the actuator for
about $2$\,s. With a clamp alone, $G_{CH}$ and $G_{CYH}$ alike wind up, overshooting
$71\%$ with a $10$\,s relay-like recovery. Back-calculating into the integrator alone
releases the rail but leaves an $18\%$ standing error that unwinds extremely slowly. Here, $k_I = 1.8\times10^{-3}$
while $R(0) = 42$, so the lag states of $R$ hold most of the low-frequency gain and
wind up on their own. For some plants and controllers, being able to split the integrator
separately to allow for exact integrator anti-windup works well on its own. For our
drivetrain plant and controller, it is better to condition the full state with the general Hanus law
\citep{hanus1987conditioning}, $x_{k+1} = A_d x_k + B_d e_k + L\,(u_{\mathrm{sat},k}-u_k)$,
which leaves the linear controller unchanged off the clamp and de-winds every state on it, achieving
$1.3\%$ overshoot and settling $2.2$\,s after the step. $L$ is pole-placed rather than
the self-conditioned $B_d/D_d$, because Tustin's method gives Equation \ref{eq:split}
a $(z+1)$ numerator factor that puts a saturated-mode eigenvalue at exactly $z=-1$.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \includegraphics[width=1\linewidth]{antiwindup_study/figures/YH-AW-18.pdf}
    \caption{Saturated step response of the drivetrain system (reference in light
    gray): clamp only, integrator-only back-calculation on the realization of
    Equation \ref{eq:split}, and full-state conditioning.}
    \label{fig:YH-AW-18}
\end{figure}

Full-state conditioning applies equally to $G_{CH}$, and the two controllers then
saturate and recover identically. The integrator separation that Youla-H allows for
makes back-calculation anti-windup simple only when the integrator is all or most of
the low-frequency gain, which is not the case for our current scenario but is often
the case in other control applications. In our case, the separation primarily improves
the elegance of the controller realization.
