Figure \ref{fig:YH-AW-2nd} applies this to the example plant of Section
\ref{sec:Alternate} with an actuator limit $|u|\le 1.5$: the reference is held at
$3$, beyond the actuator's reach, for $2.5$\,s and then dropped to $1$. With a clamp
alone, $G_{CH}$ and $G_{CYH}$ alike stay on the rail for $6.5$\,s after the
step-down and take $11$\,s to settle. Back-calculating into the integrator alone
releases the rail at once but undershoots to $0.60$: here $k_I = 0.96$ and
$R(0) = 0.93$, so the lag states of $R$ hold as much low-frequency gain as the
integrator and wind up on their own. Conditioning the full state with the general
Hanus law \citep{hanus1987conditioning},
$x_{k+1} = A_d x_k + B_d e_k + L\,(u_{\mathrm{sat},k}-u_k)$, leaves the linear
controller unchanged off the clamp and de-winds every state on it: no undershoot,
settled $2.9$\,s after the step-down. As on the drivetrain, the same conditioning
applied to $G_{CH}$ recovers identically; the Youla-H gain adjustment changes the DC
tracking, not the saturated-mode dynamics.
