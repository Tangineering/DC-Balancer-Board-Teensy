## Drive gain from the closed-loop 'V' runs — MEASURED (2026-08-16b, ML0136–ML0139, fw v11)

> ⚠️ The "implied K_F" figures and the K_v = 1.25 model treatment in this section are
> RETIRED — the contradiction they document is resolved by the 2026-08-16c correction
> section, which re-derives both evidence ratios in the corrected axis.

The first closed-loop velocity runs were made to validate the drive controller. They
failed to do that — every run limit cycled — but the +12 A rail excursions inside the
limit cycle are clean open-loop acceleration ramps, and they are the **first end-to-end
measurement of the drive gain** `K_F/m_eff` on this vehicle.

### The runs

Four manual logs, step sizes 0.1 / 0.5 / 1.0 m/s. All four **limit cycled at 2.3–2.6 Hz**
(14.5–16.3 rad/s) — the drive design's crossover — with the current alternating between
the ±12 A rails. Diagnosis: the firmware velocity estimator was a **≈113 ms boxcar**
(≈56 ms group delay = 52–58° of phase at 16 rad/s, against the design's 49.6° phase
margin; measured command→speed lag 63–73 ms; 0.0177 m/s quantization), and that element
was **absent from the synthesis plant**. Anti-windup (Hanus) was **confirmed working**:
`u_unsat` hugged the rail, ≤ 0.4 A typical excess, clean releases, ≈150 saturation
episodes. The estimator is being replaced in firmware and is now modelled explicitly in
`plant_mimo.py` (`Td_est`, see `mimo_system_model.md` §4.2).

### Acceleration fits

Fitted from the decoded CSVs (`logs/ML0137/`, `logs/ML0139/`), on the samples where
`I_cmd ≥ 11.5 A`, as `dv_act/dt` divided by the mean commanded current:

| Log | Segment | Window | Fit span | a/I [(m/s²)/A] |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ML0137 | 0 → 1.03 m/s | full (0.464 s) | v 0.00–1.03 | 0.196 |
| ML0137 | same | last 50 % | v 0.46–1.03 | **0.204** |
| ML0139 | 0 → 0.53 m/s | full (0.279 s) | v 0.00–0.53 | 0.164 |
| ML0139 | same | last 50 % | v 0.19–0.53 | **0.183** |
| ML0139 | same | last 40 % | v 0.27–0.53 | **0.186** |

**Two datapoints, reconciled.** The operator's figures were 0.158 (ML0139) and ≈0.198
(ML0137), and the refit above reproduces both from the full-window fits (0.164 / 0.196).
The ≈20 % gap between them is **not** a drag difference — drag would have made the
*faster* run the *lower* one, and it is the higher one. It is a **window-length artifact
of the retired boxcar estimator**: `v_act` reads 0 until the ≈113 ms boxcar fills, so
every from-standstill fit is depressed by a fixed ≈90 ms of apparent dead time, and that
fixed cost is a larger fraction of ML0139's 0.279 s window than of ML0137's 0.464 s. A
two-point solve for that dead time gives 93 ms, consistent with the boxcar length.
Excluding the startup transient removes the discrepancy directly: the two runs converge to
**0.186–0.204 (m/s²)/A**, and that band, not 0.158, is what the model should be centred
on.

**Discarded as unusable:** the *mid-motion* rail segments (already moving, ~150 ms long)
fit 0.087–0.103 (m/s²)/A, far below everything else. At a 2.5 Hz limit cycle a 150 ms
window is comparable to the estimator lag itself, so those fits measure the lag, not the
acceleration. Only the long from-standstill ramps are used.

### What it implies, and the contradiction it exposes

Adding the modelled drag back (`F_c` = 1.2 N plus `b_eff·v` at the fit speeds) converts the
net acceleration to a gross force constant:

- **ramps → `K_F` ≈ 0.805 N/A = ×1.78 the modelled 0.4516 N/A**
- **cruise hold (4.5 ± 0.4 A at 2.0 m/s) → `K_F` ≈ 0.409 N/A = ×0.91**

The two disagree by a **factor of ≈2, in opposite directions about the model**. No single
constant in the chain closes it: `η_dt` would have to exceed 1.0, which is not an
efficiency. **`m_eff` ≈ 1.6–2.0 kg would close it** — and that is independently consistent
with this record's own coast-down entry, whose ×1.4–1.5 observed-vs-predicted deceleration
residual "at face value would alternatively fit `m_eff` ≈ 2.4 kg". Three observations
(ramp gain, cruise current, coast-down decel) all point the same way; against them stands
one direct measurement (J = 0.0203 kg·m²).

**`m_eff` is NOT changed on that basis.** Three inferences do not outrank a direct
measurement, and re-deriving the inertia from the drag law would make the drag law and the
inertia mutually dependent. It is recorded as the top open item instead.

## K_F force-axis correction (2026-08-16c) — the m_eff investigation reports

This section closes the `m_eff` ruling above. The discrepancy was in the **force axis**,
not in the inertia. `K_F` was wrong on two independent counts, and correcting both
reconciles every drive-channel measurement in this record with `m_eff` = 3.5 kg.

### 1. The gear ratio: φ = 9.49 → 6.86

The retired 9.49 was a **stock-gearing** figure taken from the VESC integration document
(§3), which describes the vehicle as delivered. The fitted pinion is not the stock one.

Three independent sources agree on 6.86:

| Source | Reading |
|---|---|
| Traxxas 4-Tec manual p.24 formula, (spur/pinion)×2.85, with the **counted** 70T spur / 29T pinion | 70/29 × 2.85 = **6.88** |
| The same manual's ratio chart, cell (29, 70) | 2.41 pre-transmission (×2.85 → 6.87) |
| Operator rolling counts, shaft/tire stage | 2.84–2.86 (against the formula's 2.85) |

**6.86** is adopted.

**The apparent contradiction from the spin count is a VESC Tool display artifact.** The
operator's flywheel-vs-motor spin measurement read ≈32, which is ×2.02 the chain-predicted
`φ·r_fly/r_t` = 6.86 × (0.0762/0.033) = 15.8. VESC Tool's RPM display reads **twice** the
true mechanical speed (a pole/pole-pair display convention), so a commanded 1200 rpm was
actually ≈600 rpm. This is a **display artifact only** and does not touch `k_t`: the
λ-vs-KV cross-check already recorded in this file (`λ_pred` = 1.451 mWb against the
measured 1.422 mWb at p = 2) is independent of the display and confirms
`k_t` = 4.266e-3 N·m/A unchanged.

### 2. The force radius: 0.0762 m → 0.033 m

The rig chain is **motor → gear reduction 6.86 → tire (r = 0.033 m) → roller contact →
flywheel (r = 0.0762 m)**, with tire surface speed = flywheel surface speed = `v_actual`.

Two radii, two roles, previously conflated:

- **Force / motor-speed conversion uses the TIRE radius.** Motor torque reaches the road
  through the gearbox and the tire, so `K_F = k_t·η_dt·φ/r_tire` and
  `ω/v = φ/r_tire` = 207.9 rad/s per (m/s).
- **Encoder pitch and inertia use the FLYWHEEL radius.** The encoder disc *is* the
  flywheel and the measured `J` is the flywheel's, so the slot pitch (2π·0.0762/120) and
  `J/r²` both use 0.0762 m.

The velocity chain is **unaffected** — `FLYWHEEL_RADIUS_M` = 0.0762 m in firmware remains
correct, and `v_actual` remains flywheel surface speed.

### 3. The corrected constant and what rescales with it

```
K_F = k_t·η_dt·φ/r_tire = 4.266e-3 × 0.85 × 6.86 / 0.033 = 0.7538 N/A
      (equivalently k_t·η_dt × 15.8 / 0.0762 — the same chain expressed at the flywheel)
retired: 4.266e-3 × 0.85 × 9.49 / 0.0762 = 0.4516 N/A       correction ×1.669
```

**The drag law rescales with it, and `i_m0` does not move.** `b_eff` and `F_c` were both
derived *from* measured hold **currents** *through* `K_F`; the currents are the raw data
and are unchanged, so only their force conversion moved:

| Constant | Retired | Corrected |
|---|---|---|
| `b_eff` (local `dF/dv` at 2.0 m/s) | 0.32 N·s/m ±15 % | **0.534 N·s/m** ±15 % |
| `F_c` (Coulomb) | 1.2 ± 0.25 N | **2.00 ± 0.42 N** (cold 2.19 / warm 1.75–1.84) |
| `i_m0` at the nominal OP | 4.07 A | **4.07 A — invariant** |
| drive pole `−b_eff/m_eff` | −0.0914 rad/s | **−0.1526 rad/s** |

`i_m0` is invariant because drag is current-referenced: `F` and `K_F` carry the same
×1.669 and their ratio does not move. The ≈9 % shortfall against the measured
4.5 ± 0.4 A cruise hold therefore **stands unchanged**, and so does its attribution to the
unmeasured `η_dt` = 0.85.

### 4. Every contradiction in this record closes

**The ramp-vs-cruise gain contradiction (§"What it implies") dissolves.** Recomputed
against `K_F` = 0.7538 N/A and the corrected drag law:

| Evidence | Implied `K_F` | Ratio |
