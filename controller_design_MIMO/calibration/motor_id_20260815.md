## Derived quantities

- **`k_t` = (3/2)·p·λ = 1.5 × 2 × 1.422e-3 = 4.266e-3 N·m/A** (q-axis peak-current
  convention — the current `setCurrent()` commands). Replaces the 5.457e-3 placeholder
  (KV 1750): ×0.78, inside the `K_v ∈ {0.5, 2}` corner axis.
- **Pole-pair cross-check:** λ_predicted = 60/(√3·2π·KV·p) = 60/(√3·2π·1900·2)
  = 1.451 mWb vs 1.422 mWb measured (2.0 % off) → p = 2 confirmed; KV 1900 consistent.
- **`R_m` = 22.6 mΩ measured** (replaces the 0.075 Ω spec-can placeholder; was a 4.5 %
  sensitivity item, now exact).
- **VESC current-loop bandwidth implied by the detection gains:** VESC sets
  KP = L·α, KI = R·α. From KP: α = 0.0110/10.96e-6 = 1004 rad/s; check via KI:
  0.0226 × 1004 = 22.7 ≈ 22.60 ✓. So the FOC current loop is tuned for
  **τ ≈ 1/α ≈ 1.0 ms** — the VESC Tool default 1000 µs time constant, and exactly the
  `τ_v` design placeholder. The bench step test (below) should confirm ~1 ms.

## Td_v — DECIDED, not measured (operator decision 2026-08-15)

**Td_v = 2 ms nominal, corners {1, 4} ms retained** — the same values the checked-in
synthesis used. Rationale: the analytic decomposition bounds the true value at
~0.9–2 ms (UART frame 781 µs + VESC packet-thread processing ≲ 1 ms + FOC pickup
≤ ~70 µs), well inside the corner axis; every drive corner is gate-checked stable with
PM > 45° and delay margin > 4 ms, so measurement buys nothing unless the corners were
to be tightened for bandwidth. Direct measurement was declined to avoid placing a
current instrument in the motor power path (P3 pre-charge inrush + disconnect-under-
load hazard). Backstop: the small-signal `'V'` step validation against the design step
response would expose a delay assumption error as excess phase/overshoot.

## b_eff — MEASURED via steady-state ladder (2026-08-16, TP0125–TP0134, fw 8)

Ten trapezoid holds (3.5–5.5 A), all decode-clean, no faults, V_bus ≥ 15.85 V
throughout. Full fit + per-log analysis in the 2026-08-16 session record; drag-curve
figure `drag_curve_20260816.png` and pair table `drag_points_20260816.csv` alongside
this file. Force conversion `K_F = k_t·φ·η_dt/r_t = 0.4516 N/A` (model §4.2
convention — η_dt inside the force constant, still the 0.85 placeholder; all absolute
forces scale with it).

- **Drag curve is strongly concave (Stribeck-like), not viscous.** Best fits:
  `F = 1.31 N + 0.435·v` (Coulomb+viscous) and `F = 1.751·v^0.30`; pure viscous is
  excluded (χ² ×1400). Static breakaway 3.2 A → 1.445 N vs kinetic intercept 1.31 N
  (ratio 1.11, plausible stiction margin — mutually confirming).
- **`b_eff` (local dF/dv at v₀ = 2.0 m/s) = 0.32 N·s/m, ±15 %** (band set by the 8 %
  4.0 A repeat disagreement TP0126/TP0131, not formal fit errors; models B and C agree
  to 6 %). Keep v₀ = 2.0 m/s — flattest region of the curve. Below ~1.5 m/s the local
  slope roughly doubles (plant is amplitude-dependent there).
- **Coulomb term must enter the model:** populate the §4.2 `C_rr·m·g` slot with a Coulomb
  force. **SUPERSEDED by the ML0135 section below** on two points, kept for the record:
  (a) the value is **1.2 ± 0.25 N** (thermal spread), not the cold-session 1.31 N; (b) this
  bullet's `i_m0` = 4.82 A was computed with the ladder's *fitted viscous* coefficient
  0.435 N·s/m, not with the adopted **local slope 0.32 N·s/m** — at the adopted constants
  the cold figure is **4.32 A** and the nominal is **4.07 A**. The drive pole quoted here
  as ≈ −0.087 rad/s is likewise the 0.435-slope value; at `b_eff` = 0.32 and
  `m_eff` = 3.5 kg it is **−0.0914 rad/s**. Unchanged and confirmed: `i_m0` rises from
  0.973 A by a factor of ~4 (propagating into the §4.4 coupling gain `A_ω`), and
  `G22(0) = K_F/b_eff ≈ 1.41 (m/s)/A`. (§4.2's published 3.7085 / 575.2 rad/s were already
  stale on k_t and r_t.)
- **Corner axis:** {0.5, 2} centred on 0.32 no longer covers the evidence — the
  transient-fit estimate (below) reaches 0.67–1.00. Recommendation: widen to
  `pole_factor ∈ {0.5, 3}` pending resolution.
- **Coast-down (serial-plotter capture, 0.61 → 0.02 m/s, linear): RECONCILED after an
  axis correction (operator catch, 2026-08-16).** The Serial Plotter x-axis is SAMPLE
  COUNT, not seconds; at the 50 Hz `'L'` stream the decay spans ~1 s, not 49 s. An
  initial analysis misread it as seconds, concluded ×36 less drag than the ladder, and
  (wrongly) inferred the flywheel was decoupled — withdrawn. Corrected: observed decel
  ≈ 0.62 m/s² and visibly linear vs ladder-predicted 0.37–0.45 m/s² near-constant
  (Coulomb-dominated) — shape and magnitude consistent with a COUPLED drivetrain,
  independently confirming the Coulomb-dominated drag picture. Residual ×1.4–1.5
  (observed high) is within the η_dt-placeholder / eyeballed-endpoint / plotter-period
  (>20 ms by design, drifts slow) stack; at face value it would alternatively fit
  m_eff ≈ 2.4 kg — noted, not concluded. Start plateau matches the 3.5 A runs
  (0.695 m/s); no 3.25 A run exists in the set. Caution for future captures: never
  integrate time off the Serial Plotter axis — use BLG t_us or a timestamped terminal
  capture.
- **UNRESOLVED (blocks the pole number):** driven startup transients fit τ ≈ 3.5–5.2 s
  → b ≈ 0.67–1.00 N·s/m with m = 3.5 kg, vs the static slope 0.30–0.44 — a factor ≈ 2
  no single static drag law explains (thermal creep during holds is the suspect; holds
  above 4.5 A were not settled — TP0133/34 values are lower bounds).

## τ_v — MEASURED (2026-08-16, VESC Tool Sampled Data)

Current step, 3.5 A commanded (settles ~3.5–3.6 A on the MC-filtered trace), captured
at full sample rate, rotor initially at standstill.
Filtered total current: 63 % at ≈ 1–1.5 ms, ~95 % by 3–4 ms — first-order with
**τ_v ≈ 1.0–1.5 ms**, matching the 1.0 ms implied by the detection gains (KP/L = KI/R
≈ 1004 rad/s). Raw phase currents show the same initial constant (not a display-filter
artifact). Rotor broke away ~11 ms into the capture (step was above the 3.2 A
breakaway); the current loop held its setpoint through rotation onset. **Adopted:
τ_v = 1.0 ms nominal; corners {0.5, 5} ms unchanged** (measurement is comfortably
interior; pessimistic read ≤ 2 ms).

## Small-signal steps — MEASURED (2026-08-16, ML0135, fw 9 manual log)

Up-down staircase 0 → 4.0 → 4.5 → 5.0 → 4.75 → 4.25 → 4.0 → 0 A, 115.7 s at 1 kHz,
decode-clean (0 faults, V_bus ≥ 15.84 V, v_act to 3.22 m/s). Five incremental steps,
exponential fits over 12 s post-step windows:

| step | Δv (m/s) | τ (s) | dF/dv (N·s/m) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4.00→4.50 | +0.616 | 5.1 | 0.366 |
| 4.50→5.00 | +0.956 | 8.8 | 0.236 |
| 5.00→4.75 | −0.261 | 11.9 | 0.433 |
| 4.75→4.25 | −0.760 | 7.7 | 0.297 |
| 4.25→4.00 | −0.387 | 6.3 | 0.292 |

- **b_eff CONFIRMED: mean small-signal dF/dv = 0.32 ± 0.07 N·s/m** over 1.9–3.4 m/s —
  matches the TP-ladder slope exactly. Adopted value stands.
- **The factor-2 transient puzzle DISSOLVES.** m/τ across the incremental steps spans
  0.29–0.69 N·s/m (mean ≈ 0.47), scattered on BOTH sides of the static slope — the
  clean ×2 from the startup-transient fits was a Coulomb + thermal-drift artifact,
  not a distinct dynamic damping. Corner {0.5, 3} retained as prudence; revisit after
  re-synthesis if it costs performance.
- **The Coulomb term is thermally variable — the main insight.** At 4.0 A this (warm)
  session holds 1.88 m/s ascending / 2.03 m/s descending (+8 % within-session) vs
  0.99–1.07 m/s in the colder TP0125–0134 session, with NO firmware scale change
  (fw 9 = logging only). The slope is session-invariant; the intercept fits at
  F_c ≈ 1.05–1.1 N warm vs 1.31 N cold (−20 %). Retroactively explains the TP holds'
  monotone creep and the 8 % TP0126/TP0131 repeat gap. **Model treatment:
  F_c = 1.2 ± 0.25 N (thermal spread)** — carry the spread into the §4.4 coupling-gain
  corner rather than a point value. **Measured** hold current at v₀ = 2.0 m/s is
  **4.5 ± 0.4 A**. Note this does *not* equal what the adopted constants predict: with
  `b_eff` = 0.32 N·s/m and `K_F` = 0.4516 N/A the model gives **4.07 A** nominal, with a
  thermal envelope of **3.74 A warm (F_c 1.05) to 4.32 A cold (F_c 1.31)**. (An earlier
  draft of this bullet quoted "cold 4.82, warm ≈ 4.1"; 4.82 used the fitted viscous slope
  0.435 rather than the adopted local slope, and 4.1 does not reproduce from any warm
  `F_c` in the stated range.) The model therefore runs ~9 % below the measured band's
  centre and just under its lower edge — consistent with the unmeasured `η_dt` = 0.85,
  which scales all absolute forces.
