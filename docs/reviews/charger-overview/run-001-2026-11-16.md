## Round 1 (verbatim)

Needs major edits.

1. **Major — The hardware-independence claim contradicts the design record.**  
   **OVERVIEW.md:100, 111:** “four independent layers”; “The permit gate is the only layer that does not depend on correct firmware or correct register values.”  
   This suggests a general hardware safety guarantee. The authoritative `docs/charger_bq25798_wiring_design_2026-11-05.md:75` explicitly says the FC-window loop barrier is **firmware only**. Lines **85–87** also state that the ILIM_HIZ inhibit depends on `EN_EXTILIM = 1`; independence from that register is unverified. `README.md:226–234` distinguishes protection by hazard.  
   **Fix:** Replace the independence claim with: “Protection varies by mode. During regen-armed driving, hardware checks the motor-to-bus voltage relationship. During FC charging, firmware must disconnect the battery boost from VBUS. Register-independent operation of the input inhibit remains a bench-verification item.”

2. **Major — “Released” conflates three different states.**  
   **OVERVIEW.md:115–116, 143, 150:** “converts power only when two hardware pins allow it”; “‘Released’ means that the permit gate lets the BQ25798 convert”; “charging disabled … Briefly … for the start-up check.”  
   A reader cannot distinguish input power present, ILIM_HIZ released, and battery charging enabled. Qualification deliberately releases ILIM_HIZ while charging remains disabled. `README.md:244–246` includes the additional `EN_CHG` register condition; **355–358** explains qualification’s small quiescent draw. The design record **2660** specifies `/CE HIGH` and `EN_CHG = 0` during qualification.  
   **Fix:** Define these three states separately. Give Table 3 separate columns for **input source**, **ILIM release**, and **battery charging permitted**. State explicitly that qualification does not charge the pack.

3. **Major — Table 3’s “Never” release entries contradict the equations and authoritative mode table.**  
   **OVERVIEW.md:149, 153:** “Init, Idle … Never”; “Fault (State 99), Teensy reset … Never.”  
   The equation at line 131 permits `H1 AND UVB` regardless of CHG_ARM. The design record **2658–2659, 2673–2674** explicitly corrects earlier claims of zero release: ILIM_REL can be HIGH in these modes, while `/CE` and lack of input power prevent charging.  
   **Fix:** Say “No battery charging; input paths off and CHG_ARM LOW. ILIM_REL may still follow H1 AND UVB.” Do not describe gate release as impossible.

4. **Major — The diagram removes the switch on which the FC-window safety argument depends.**  
   **OVERVIEW.md:50:** `PACK["2S pack"] --> BTB["BT boost"] --> VBUS`  
   This draws an uninterrupted battery-to-bus path. Later, lines **107 and 152** require a battery-to-bus switch to be OFF, but the reader has never seen it. `README.md:371–373` says opening BT_BUS removes the pack-to-VBUS path; the design record **2665** identifies this as the FC-window loop barrier. The permit-gate diagram also omits the FC_CHARGE-enable sense that supplies two of its six inputs.  
   **Fix:** Add **BT_BUS** between battery boost and VBUS, and add an **FC_CHARGE enable sense** connection to the permit gate. These are essential explanatory elements, not optional connector detail.

5. **Major — The reason FC charging needs windows is missing.**  
   **OVERVIEW.md:20, 152:** “in the gaps between fuel-cell purges”; “the window closes before each fuel-cell purge.”  
   Readers are told the scheduling rule without its physical cause. They need to connect battery isolation, FC-only bus support, and the FC output collapse during purge. `README.md:366–373` requires a locked purge schedule and holds BT_BUS LOW throughout the window; the design record **2863–2878** describes the measured purge and predictive handoff.  
   **Fix:** Add: “To prevent circular charging, the battery boost is disconnected from VBUS during FC charging. The fuel cell therefore supports the bus alone. Before its periodic purge collapses its output, firmware stops FC charging and restores battery support.” Distinguish a **predicted** purge deadline from a guaranteed known onset.

6. **Major — The watchdog reads as an implemented, verified guarantee.**  
   **OVERVIEW.md:109:** “A Teensy hardware watchdog resets the controller within about 50 ms if the firmware hangs; the reset drops every switch…”  
   Section 7 eventually says this firmware does not exist yet. Moreover, `README.md:377–382` qualifies the approximately 51 ms shutdown bound by an unverified pad-keeper condition and notes the purge-coincident residual. The design record **83** states the same limitation.  
   **Fix:** Use future tense: “The planned watchdog targets a 50 ms reset. Charging requires its forced-hang tests to pass; shutdown also depends on verifying that the command pins fall LOW at reset.”

7. **Major — The reset explanation incorrectly says every gate input becomes LOW.**  
   **OVERVIEW.md:137–138:** “At reset, at power-up and while the comparators start, every input reads LOW…”  
   MCU reset and comparator power-on reset are different events. Powered comparators can continue reporting HIGH during a Teensy reset. `README.md:301–303` separates command pull-downs from comparator startup; the design record **2175 and 2674** does likewise.  
   **Fix:** “At MCU reset, CHG_ARM and CHG_QUAL default LOW and the charger-path enables fall LOW. During comparator power-on reset, the four comparator outputs are LOW.” Explain the resulting inhibit without claiming all six inputs always reset together.

8. **Major — The comparator descriptions turn hysteretic conditions into incorrect single thresholds.**  
   **OVERVIEW.md:122–123, 151:** “V-MOT is more than 8.9 % above VBUS”; “VBUS is healthy (above about 13.3 V)”; “Only while V-MOT exceeds the bus.”  
   The last wording can imply that any positive voltage difference enables harvesting. UVB’s quoted 13.3 V is its falling threshold, not its turn-on threshold. `README.md:257–258` gives H1 rise/fall examples of **17.31/17.01 V at VBUS 15.9 V**, and UVB **13.72 V rising / 13.33 V falling**.  
   **Fix:** Label nominal rising and falling thresholds, or explicitly say “hysteretic comparator.” Make Table 3 reference **H1 AND UVB**, not merely “exceeds the bus.” Also distinguish gate permission from the approximately 19 V input-regulation floor required for actual regen draw.

9. **Major — FC_CHARGE command sensing becomes a claim of proven conduction.**  
   **OVERVIEW.md:132, 152:** “the FC_CHARGE switch is really on”; “the FC_CHARGE switch is proven on.”  
   The comparator senses the enable voltage, not switch conduction. The input table itself is more accurate at line 124. The design record **1691** says “guaranteed commanded on”; `README.md:260` likewise says “commanded on.”  
   **Fix:** Consistently write “the FC_CHARGE enable voltage is valid HIGH.” Explicitly say this is command-level validation, not current-flow feedback.

10. **Major — The numbered next steps put bench testing before its firmware prerequisite.**  
    **OVERVIEW.md:226–227:** “Modify the EVM and the MB, then run the bench tests…” followed by “Write and test the charger firmware, starting with the watchdog.”  
    A newcomer following this sequence reaches powered commissioning before the firmware that lines **201–202** require. `README.md:397–405` requires watchdog qualification before charger arming and the new pin-map firmware before connecting the EVM.  
    **Fix:** Split passive assembly checks from powered commissioning. Develop and qualify the required firmware before integration tests that connect or arm the charger. Mention that FC windows additionally require purge-emulator testing.

11. **Major — Fault shutdown is described as an instantaneous all-switches-off state.**  
    **OVERVIEW.md:153:** “Fault (State 99), Teensy reset | All switches off.”  
    This collapses controlled fault teardown and hardware reset into one mechanism. `README.md:347–348` distinguishes them: State 99 inhibits charging before switches move; reset drops enables. The design record **2673** specifies phased teardown.  
    **Fix:** Separate the rows: “Fault: charging inhibited immediately, followed by ordered switch teardown”; “MCU reset: enables default LOW.”

12. **Major — Figure 2’s header description is factually wrong.**  
    **OVERVIEW.md:164–165:** “The 22-pin header runs down the left edge…”  
    J1 consists of two separated pin columns, not one 22-pin row along that edge. `README.md:511–513` identifies the **10-position and 12-position strips**. The supplied image shows both columns.  
    **Fix:** “The two J1 pin columns mate with the former Ag105 footprint; the eFuse occupies the upper region and the permit logic the lower central region.” Annotate the image so readers can identify them.

13. **Minor — First-stop questions on a newcomer reread: the title, then “purge.” — Readability judgment.**  
    **OVERVIEW.md:1:** “Charger Retrofit (CR / EIB / EVM): Design Overview.”  
    This is the literal first point where I would ask what the three labels mean and whether they name alternatives or assemblies. If I defer that question, the first substantive stop is line **20**, “fuel-cell purges”: its definition arrives only at **246**.  
    **Fix:** Title it “Fuel-cell vehicle charger retrofit.” Introduce CR, EIB and EVM with Table 1. Define purge at line 20 as periodic venting that briefly collapses fuel-cell output.

14. **Minor — Other first-use terms and identifier families need small local explanations. — Readability judgment.**  
    **OVERVIEW.md:11, 50–66, 106, 131, 192:** “2S”; “BT boost”; “VCHG-IN”; “CHG_ARM, CHG_QUAL”; “regen-armed”; “ILIM_REL”; “ERC 0, DRC 0.”  
    VBUS, V-MOT and VESC are adequately introduced in the opening. These others are either introduced later or never explicitly decoded. P5 and QUAL have local aliases, but switching between aliases and command names makes the equations harder to follow.  
    **Fix:** Define “2S = two cells in series,” “BT = battery,” and “regen-armed = input path powered and ready, but charging inhibited until regen qualifies.” Label ILIM_REL as the Boolean input-inhibit release signal. Use CHG_ARM/CHG_QUAL consistently. Explain U/J/W/E once as component, connector and lead identifiers; expand ERC/DRC if retained.

15. **Minor — Figure 1’s line legend contradicts its source.**  
    **OVERVIEW.md:44, 49–51:** “thin lines carry logic,” but the fuel-cell, battery and motor power paths use `-->`.  
    The diagram therefore encodes several power paths as logic. It also combines the motor-side electrical node and motor in “V-MOT / motor,” obscuring regenerative energy flow.  
    **Fix:** Use thick arrows for all depicted power paths, thin arrows for commands, and dotted arrows for sensing. Separate V-MOT from the VESC/motor and show bidirectional power between them. The Mermaid source has no obvious syntax defect on inspection, but rendering was **not verified**: no Mermaid renderer was available locally. Check the actual presentation renderer or embed a pre-rendered SVG.

16. **Minor — “One of two switches” suggests mutually exclusive operation. — Readability judgment.**  
    **OVERVIEW.md:80:** “One of two MB switches routes power…”  
    Table 3 later has both FC_CHARGE and REGEN ON. The missing explanation is ideal-diode source selection, not an error in that FC-window row. `README.md:371–373` explains that motor voltage during braking reverse-biases the FC charger path.  
    **Fix:** “Two ideal-diode paths feed the common charger input. Both may be enabled during an FC window; when V-MOT rises above VBUS, the motor supplies the charger and the FC path blocks reverse current.”

17. **Minor — The overview gives protection current but omits useful charging capability. — Readability judgment.**  
    **OVERVIEW.md:84–86:** “limits the input current to about 2.4 A”; “converts it to the pack charge voltage.”  
    The only prominent current is the eFuse fault limit. A lab audience will ask for normal input limits, pack-current targets and voltage target, and may mistake 2.4 A for charging current. The design record **2645–2650** gives regen input **1.70 A**, FC input **≤0.90 A**, FC charge current **1.60 A**, and regen charge current **2.50 A pending qualification for 3.10 A**. `README.md:229` gives the interim **8.320 V** target.  
    **Fix:** Add a compact operating-limits table with separate **input current**, **battery charge current**, **voltage target**, and **qualification status** columns. Include the EIB’s mode-dependent current clamp, omitted from the overview’s gate description (`README.md:276–299`).

18. **Minor — Nominal eFuse thresholds look like guaranteed boundaries.**  
    **OVERVIEW.md:84–85:** “about 2.4 A … below about 9.9 V and above about 23.2 V.”  
    The nominal values are reasonable, but “disconnects below” hides hysteresis, and “about” does not convey the significant tolerance range. `README.md:76` gives UVLO **rising** 9.86 V and OVP **rising** 23.19 V, with leakage-inclusive ranges **9.055–10.548 V** and **21.588–25.019 V**.  
    **Fix:** Label the values “typical” and distinguish UVLO turn-on from turn-off. Add a short operational consequence: hard braking can trip the input protection and interrupt harvesting; that behavior is accepted (`README.md:1430`).

19. **Minor — The open-item summary hides limitations more consequential than pad layout. — Readability judgment.**  
    **OVERVIEW.md:229–232:** “One layout question is open…”  
    This does not literally claim all other issues are closed, but it leaves the audience with that impression after “complete” at line 221. The full guide retains questions about comparator-tap interruptions, eFuse recovery and firmware overvoltage behavior (`README.md:1431–1434`). The design record **76–87** also qualifies pack protection and inhibit independence.  
    **Fix:** Add a brief “Not yet demonstrated” paragraph covering the key electrical/firmware validation dependencies and accepted hard-brake dropout. Keep the W5 manufacturing question subordinate to those system-level limitations.

20. **Minor — Reordering W5 is presented as an alternative that could eliminate the test.**  
    **OVERVIEW.md:231–232:** “The options are a staged continuity check after soldering or a reordered pad grid.”  
    The design record **4225–4227** says no grid order makes every adjacent pair benign and a reorder **still needs the post-solder test**.  
    **Fix:** “The open choice is whether to retain or reorder the grid; either layout requires post-solder isolation checks.” Also identify the lost barrier: a bridge can force `/CE` LOW during qualification, leaving `EN_CHG = 0` as the remaining charging inhibit (**4216–4218**).

21. **Minor — The interconnect table drops a signal and obscures several mappings.**  
    **OVERVIEW.md:180–184:** “three readbacks”; “W5 … Permit-gate outputs, 3.3 V, ground”; “W6, W7 … Optional pack thermistor; I2C.”  
    W5 also carries charger status back toward the CR (`README.md:100`). The three readbacks are unnamed, and readers must infer which of W6/W7 is optional.  
    **Fix:** Add STAT to W5. Name the readbacks as H1, eFuse fault and input current. Explicitly map W6 to the optional thermistor and W7 to I2C; map W1/W2 to input and W3/W4 to output.

22. **Minor — Table 5 appears exhaustive but omits a pin essential to the gate.**  
    **OVERVIEW.md:204:** “Table 5 lists the Teensy pins that the charger uses.”  
    Pin 31, FC_CHARGE_ENABLE, supplies the gate’s FC-enable sense but is absent; `README.md:420` explicitly lists it. Existing path-switch pins are also outside this table’s unstated scope.  
    **Fix:** Add pin 31 and say whether the table covers charger-interface pins only or all charger-path control pins. For useful firmware context, note that pin 5 replaces MPPT_DISABLE and pin 6’s charging-status polarity changes (`README.md:415–416`).

23. **Minor — The physical scope and implementation status are easy to misread. — Readability judgment.**  
    **OVERVIEW.md:13–14, 29–30:** “All of this sits on one printed circuit board”; “The Ag105 is removed and replaced by a stock TI BQ25798EVM”; “only parts change.”  
    “All of this” appears to include the fuel cell, battery and motor controller. “Is removed” sounds completed, despite line 221 saying nothing has been removed. “Stock” and “only parts change” understate the EVM modifications and added wiring described later.  
    **Fix:** “The boosts, power-path switches and Teensy are on the main board.” Then: “The proposed retrofit replaces the Ag105 with a commercially available BQ25798EVM, modified for this interface. Main-board traces remain intact; selected parts and interconnects change.”

24. **Minor — The Ag105 incident and loop explanation invite stronger causal conclusions than the text establishes. — Readability judgment.**  
    **OVERVIEW.md:25, 94–96:** “drew about 11 A”; “the loop can run away”; “which destroys it.”  
    The 11 A observation lacks an expected-current comparison or explicit statement about whether its cause was established. “Run away” does not explain that this is externally supplied circulating power and increasing component stress, rather than energy amplification. “Destroys” reads as inevitable.  
    **Fix:** Separate observed incident from diagnosed incompatibilities. Explain that the loop needlessly recirculates source power through lossy converters and can cause excessive current and heating. Use “can damage” for the back-feed consequence unless a specific demonstrated event is being described.

25. **Polish — References are present, but navigation and figure claims need tightening.**  
    **OVERVIEW.md:73–74, 139:** “The complete diagram, with every connector and pin”; bare backticked figure paths.  
    All three referenced image files exist. However, the larger block diagram is not a pin-complete schematic, and the two backticked paths are not clickable links.  
    **Fix:** Say “A more detailed interconnect diagram is available here,” link it, and link the gate figure and source documents. Retain the schematic as the pin-level authority.

26. **Polish — Mechanical detail crowds out the operating explanation. — Readability judgment.**  
    **OVERVIEW.md:159–194:** “Samtec SSQ sockets”; “four 6-32 holes”; “1 oz outer / 0.5 oz inner”; “Order quantity | 6 | 3.”  
    These details occupy substantial space before the reader learns that the firmware is unimplemented. Meanwhile, charging capability, qualification purpose and purge handoff remain underexplained. The rounded board sizes, mounting height and board-order quantities are consistent with the full guide; their prominence is the issue.  
    **Fix:** Move implementation status immediately after the proposed solution. Reduce physical build to mounting, external connections and one annotated image; move fabrication specifications, procurement quantities and the detailed pin table to an appendix. Reuse that space for the corrected operating-mode table and a short purge-handoff explanation.
