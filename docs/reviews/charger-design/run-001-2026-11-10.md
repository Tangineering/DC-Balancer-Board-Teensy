## Findings

1. **Adjacent FLT/enable shorts are electrically unsafe and their claimed detector is ineffective.**

   - **Severity:** major.
   - **Location:** [design:1127–1128](<C:/Life Ops/School/Thesis/DC-Balancer-round4/docs/charger_bq25798_wiring_design_2026-11-05.md:1127>), also lines 1539 and 2774. Sources: `round4/calc/r7_calc_out.txt:325,333,397` under the review directory; [TPS2663 datasheet, §6.1, p.5](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tps2663.pdf), which specifies **10 mA maximum FLT sink current**.
   - **Claim in the document:** F05–F17 or F06–F17 shorts pull the enable “only toward off” and announce themselves through FR-08.
   - **What can go wrong (with numbers):** These bridges connect an asserted open-drain FLT directly to a HIGH push-pull Teensy output. Using the calculation file’s explicitly assumed 40 Ω GPIO resistance and an illustrative 0.4 V LOW gives `(3.3 − 0.4)/40 = 72.5 mA`, **7.25 times** FLT’s absolute maximum. This is an estimate, not a guaranteed current; the missing current limit is certain. Either the enable remains insufficiently low, or the outputs contend outside a demonstrated safe envelope. Moreover, FLT LOW makes `regnExpected` false, disabling the cited monitor. FR-23’s alternative plausibility check requires fresh I²C while the same rule prohibits new I²C transactions with FLT LOW.
   - **Recommendation (concrete):** Remove these adjacencies or add protection between the FLT pin and the exposed pad, with verified GPIO/FLT current bounds. Define a fault check that remains executable with FLT LOW.
   
   **New evidence:** The 10 mA absolute maximum and the FR-23 reachability conflict invalidate these particular “accepted” adjacency rows; this is separate from the already-disposed F05–F12 and F07–F12 pairs.

2. **The mandatory register readbacks invalidate T3’s published timing budget.**

   - **Severity:** major.
   - **Location:** [design:2653](<C:/Life Ops/School/Thesis/DC-Balancer-round4/docs/charger_bq25798_wiring_design_2026-11-05.md:2653>), lines 2733–2736 and 2792. Source: `docs/reviews/charger_eval_2026-11-05/round3/calc/r3_calc_out.txt:179–180`.
   - **Claim in the document:** T3 completes within 1.5 ms, including the 100 kHz fallback; every limit write is followed by VREG and written-field readback in its queued transaction group.
   - **What can go wrong (with numbers):** The calculation counts only the two writes. Following the specified write/verify groups, the first write, three-byte VREG-area read, IINDPM read and second write require at least `4 + 6 + 5 + 5 = 20` wire bytes. Including ACK bits: `20 × 9 / 100 kHz = 1.80 ms`, before START/STOP overhead or an existing transaction. Adding the permitted 0.50 ms transaction gives **at least 2.30 ms**, versus the published 1.35 ms. Even reducing the VREG read to two bytes gives 2.21 ms. The brake-response calculation therefore uses a deadline the specified 100 kHz sequence cannot meet.
   - **Recommendation (concrete):** Publish an explicit transaction schedule and recompute the brake-response bound. Either require 400 kHz for this operating mode or specify and justify a safe readback-order exception; do not silently omit verification.

   **New evidence:** FR-46’s later verification requirement was not incorporated into the older T3 calculation. This is distinct from the settled reversal-hold timing issue.

3. **The Idle D-BT-SQ short detector cannot run through the specified I²C gate.**

   - **Severity:** major.
   - **Location:** [design:2794](<C:/Life Ops/School/Thesis/DC-Balancer-round4/docs/charger_bq25798_wiring_design_2026-11-05.md:2794>), lines 2774 and 3035. Sources: `references/Scale Car Teensy IO - IO.csv:28`; `references/Scale_Car_Board_20260624.sch:8971–8976`; [BQ25798 datasheet, §7.3.10, p.47](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/bq25798.pdf).
   - **Claim in the document:** FR-48 detects a shorted D-BT-SQ in Idle by obtaining an ACK and reading VBAT while pin-38 `V_chg < 3 V`.
   - **What can go wrong (with numbers):** FR-23 permits those transactions only when the same input measurement is **above 10.2 V**. The required conjunction, `V_chg < 3 V AND V_chg > 10.2 V`, is unreachable. A short can consequently leave W3/EVM BAT connected to the pack without the promised Idle warning. Battery-only ADC operation above 2.9 V is supported by TI; the obstacle is the document’s software gate.
   - **Recommendation (concrete):** Add an explicit, bounded battery-only diagnostic exception to FR-23. Exercise T-D-15 through the actual transaction gate, rather than injecting an ACK directly into the diagnostic.

   **New evidence:** The accepted FR-48 addition conflicts with the pre-existing FR-23 gate; the earlier disposition does not resolve that interaction.

4. **T1 step 8b has an impossible completion budget and leaves charging inhibited.**

   - **Severity:** major.
   - **Location:** [design:2651](<C:/Life Ops/School/Thesis/DC-Balancer-round4/docs/charger_bq25798_wiring_design_2026-11-05.md:2651>), lines 1328–1334. Sources: `docs/reviews/charger_eval_2026-11-05/round3/calc/r3_calc_out.txt:181–183`; [SN74LVC1G123 datasheet, §7.1, p.12](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/sn74lvc1g123.pdf).
   - **Claim in the document:** The entire second-QUAL heartbeat test completes within approximately 70 ms, including waiting for pin 33 to return LOW; step 9 says P5 “stays 1.”
   - **What can go wrong (with numbers):** Step 8b sets P5 LOW and never restores it. Lowering QUAL does not clear the monostable; its CLR remains released while P5 is LOW. After heartbeat resumes, QQ therefore persists until its **200–464 ms** pulse expires. Even the minimum exceeds 70 ms by **130 ms**. Independently, step 9 inherits P5 LOW, so writing EN_CHG = 1 does not release `/CE`.
   - **Recommendation (concrete):** Separate the ≤70 ms heartbeat exercise from the subsequent one-shot-expiry wait. Add an explicit, verified P5 re-arm transition after QUAL is LOW and the required register checks pass. Test the complete T1 sequence with both monostable timing extremes.

   **New evidence:** These are sequencing defects introduced by the accepted second-QUAL test, not a request to remove that test.

5. **The foot-bond fallback invalidates the recorded gap and stiffness qualification.**

   - **Severity:** minor.
   - **Location:** [design:922–928](<C:/Life Ops/School/Thesis/DC-Balancer-round4/docs/charger_bq25798_wiring_design_2026-11-05.md:922>), lines 894–898 and 2975. Source: `docs/reviews/charger_eval_2026-11-05/round4/calc/r7_calc_out.txt:522–527`.
   - **Claim in the document:** Failed polyimide adhesion can be repaired by removing the sheet beneath the foot, bedding onto solder mask and repeating T-A-09, while retaining the gated 0.15–0.45 mm bed basis.
   - **What can go wrong (with numbers):** The headers retain their sheet seat, but the foot loses **0.125 mm** beneath it. The previous gap becomes **0.275–0.575 mm**, nominally 0.425 mm. At the upper end, `k = EA/t` gives FT1 `6.4/0.575 = 11.13 N/mm` and FT2 `16/0.575 = 27.83 N/mm`, **21.7% below** the respective minimum stiffnesses calculated using 0.45 mm. Repeating the pull test does not make the old dry-gap record valid.
   - **Recommendation (concrete):** Require a fresh gap measurement and either taller replacement feet or a separately calculated and qualified fallback bed range.

   **New evidence:** D-F1’s accepted stack-up assumes both headers and feet stand on the sheet; the D3 fallback breaks that assumption.

6. **Supersession notation leaves conflicting assembly instructions live.**

   - **Severity:** minor.
   - **Location:** [design:501](<C:/Life Ops/School/Thesis/DC-Balancer-round4/docs/charger_bq25798_wiring_design_2026-11-05.md:501>), also line 1536. Sources: `round4/calc/r7_calc_out.txt:623–624,696–698`; `round4/review_loop.md:146`, under the review directory.
   - **Claim in the document:** Line 501 first exempts the chassis tie from the 30 mm span rule, then places the mandatory limits inside a “superseded” parenthesis. Line 1536 similarly presents “as short as possible” before the fixed-length correction.
   - **What can go wrong (with numbers):** Following the first instruction permits an unbounded final unsupported span; following the later one limits the west tie to **2.4 mm past the MB edge** with margin. For W1/W2, shortening to the approximately 199.5 mm route changes the modeled IN peak from **32.8 V to 34.4 V**, reducing the 36 V breakdown margin from 3.2 V to 1.6 V. The notation is used in both directions elsewhere, so the reader cannot apply one consistent supersession rule.
   - **Recommendation (concrete):** Leave exactly one current instruction in each assembly section. Move obsolete text into explicitly historical blocks and keep the fixed 0.30 m W1/W2 instruction unambiguous.

   This does not reopen the accepted anchor or length decisions; it identifies their contradictory presentation in the implementation record.

7. **Some r7 hand-calculated numbers remain outside the required calculation outputs.**

   - **Severity:** minor.
   - **Location:** [design:3312–3314](<C:/Life Ops/School/Thesis/DC-Balancer-round4/docs/charger_bq25798_wiring_design_2026-11-05.md:3312>), also line 604. Sources: `round4/work/evm_geometry.md:107–108`; `round4/calc/r7_calc_out.txt:594–611,616`, under the review directory.
   - **Claim in the document:** The EVM-to-CR distances are 55.8–129.4 mm; damped E8 pickup is 0.17 mV per 10 mV REGN ripple.
   - **What can go wrong (with numbers):** These results are absent from the two designated calculation-output files. The distance endpoints independently reproduce:
     `√[(39.370−0.400−37.560)² + (13.741+118.044−76.000)²] = 55.803 mm`;
     the corresponding TP49 calculation gives **129.418 mm**. The stated damped pickup approximation likewise gives `10 mV × 2π × 1.430 MHz × 19.1 nH / 10 Ω = 0.1716 mV`. These examples are numerically plausible, but their derivations are not captured where H.23 promises reproducibility.
   - **Recommendation (concrete):** Put the distance and E8 transfer calculations, assumptions and output rows into `r7_calc.py`/`r7_calc_out.txt`, including the undamped pickup range used by T-C-16.

8. **The thermal test specifies two incompatible duty cycles.**

   - **Severity:** minor.
   - **Location:** [design:2996](<C:/Life Ops/School/Thesis/DC-Balancer-round4/docs/charger_bq25798_wiring_design_2026-11-05.md:2996>), T-B-16; the related vehicle test is at line 3067.
   - **Claim in the document:** Apply 0.6 s current bursts at 1 Hz with 30% duty.
   - **What can go wrong (with numbers):** `0.6 s × 1 Hz = 60%`, not 30%. With zero current between bursts, the burst contribution to average resistive heating differs by **2×** between those interpretations. Two operators can therefore qualify different thermal loads.
   - **Recommendation (concrete):** Specify one waveform, including inter-burst current: either 0.6 s every 2 s for 30%, or 0.6 s every 1 s for 60%, and synchronize T-F-16.
